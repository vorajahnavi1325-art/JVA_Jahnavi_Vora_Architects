IN PASSING — CAMPUS KIOSK

A place to stop
without having to stop.

IN PASSING explores a compact campus kiosk as a piece of social infrastructure. Set within a narrow 3 × 12 metre strip, the project avoids separating service, seating and movement into rigid zones. Instead, overlapping circular volumes bring them together into one continuous spatial experience.

The stepped edges become places to sit, meet and wait, while the central kiosk remains open to the movement around it. Timber cladding gives the small structure a warm, tactile presence within the landscape.

PROGRAMKIOSK / SEATING

THE IDEA

From a narrow strip
to an overlapping landscape.

The starting point is simple: a long, narrow site. A conventional division between kiosk and seating would make the small footprint feel even more rigid. The design instead breaks the program into rounded volumes and allows them to overlap.

Where the circles meet, circulation, service and occupation begin to blur. The result is a kiosk that behaves less like an object and more like a small public room within the campus.

PLAN

Service at the centre.
People around it.

The plan places the kiosk and service counter at the centre of the composition. Seating expands outward on both sides, allowing people to occupy the building without blocking the flow of the campus.

The circular geometry creates a sequence of small thresholds rather than a single front and back.

PLAN / 12 000 × 3 000

SECTION A–A′

Architecture as
something to sit on.

The section makes the idea visible: the stepped perimeter is not leftover space. It becomes seating, lookout points and informal social space.

IN USE

Passing through.
Staying awhile.

JVA

THE PROJECT

Small footprint.
Shared ground.

IN PASSING treats the everyday campus stop as an opportunity for architecture to create connection. Its scale is deliberately small, but its edges are generous — giving people somewhere to pause, gather and continue.
