IN TRANSLATION — MODULAR HOUSE FAÇADE · LEN+K PRAGUE

One module,
many readings.

A façade study built from repetition, timber, glass and a small family of folded elements. The work explores how one modular language can shift between openness, privacy, depth and rhythm.

Hand sketches become a full-scale architectural vocabulary.

02 — ITERATION

Drawing the
module first.

Early studies test grids, openings, solid panels and the relationship between repeated bays. Colour marks different conditions and helps the façade move from diagram to spatial proposition.

03 — PATTERN / PROPORTION
04 — FAÇADE LANGUAGE

Repetition creates
difference.

The same underlying rhythm can be read in several ways: as a regular grid, a folded timber screen, a sequence of windows, or a field of framed rooms.

05 — MATERIAL PALETTE

Warmth meets
precision.

Timber carries the visual rhythm of the façade. Glass opens the modules to daylight, while darker metal elements give the system a quiet structural frame.

06 — DETAIL / FOLDED TIMBER
09 — MATERIAL TRANSLATION

From a surface
to a system.

The façade studies translate a simple material palette into a repeatable construction language, where diagonal grain, folded edges and framed openings give each module its own depth.

10 — MODULAR FAÇADE / EXTERIOR
11 — TRANSLATION

A small grammar
for a larger façade.

What begins as a series of hand-drawn studies becomes a consistent architectural language of bays, folds, frames and openings.
