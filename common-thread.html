COMMON THREAD — STUDENT HOUSING

Private rooms.
Shared life.

COMMON THREAD explores student housing as a network of shared experiences rather than a collection of individual rooms. Residential blocks gather around common courtyards, social spaces and a central shared facility, creating a campus where everyday encounters become part of living.

The architecture balances privacy with connection. Repeated residential modules establish a clear rhythm, while shared terraces, paths, landscape and communal interiors stitch the buildings into one collective environment.

THE COMMON GROUND

Housing becomes
a small community.

The project is organised around the idea that the most important spaces in student housing are often the ones between private rooms. Courtyards, common facilities, walkways and gathering spaces create a continuous social network.

The central shared building acts as a social anchor, while the surrounding residential blocks define a sequence of outdoor rooms. The arrangement allows students to move naturally between solitude, small-group interaction and larger communal activity.

CAMPUS ORGANISATION

Many buildings.
One shared centre.

From above, the housing reads as a series of residential wings arranged around a central common facility and landscape. Paths and planted courts create the connective tissue between the blocks.

The repeated geometry gives the larger development a calm rhythm while allowing each cluster to retain its own sense of address.

PRIVATE LIFE

A room to
return to.

The private room is deliberately calm and compact, using warm timber surfaces, integrated storage and controlled daylight to create a sense of permanence within a highly social environment.

Rather than competing with the collective spaces, the room provides a quiet counterpoint to them — a place for rest, study and retreat.

SHARED DOMESTICITY

Common spaces
feel like home.

Kitchens, lounges and informal meeting areas are treated as extensions of domestic life. Timber joinery, warm lighting and built-in storage give shared interiors a familiar scale rather than an institutional one.

The material language carries through the residential environment, allowing individual spaces to feel related even when their uses change.

LIVING TOGETHER

Rooms.
Routines.
Relationships.

THE PROJECT

A shared life,
held together.

COMMON THREAD understands student housing as more than accommodation. The architecture creates a framework for friendships, routines and informal encounters — connecting private lives through a network of shared ground.
