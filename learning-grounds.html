LEARNING GROUNDS — RURAL SCHOOL

A school built
around belonging.

LEARNING GROUNDS imagines a rural school as a cluster of connected places rather than a single institutional block. Classrooms, shared spaces, landscape and play areas are arranged around courtyards so that movement between learning and everyday life becomes part of the architecture.

Curved volumes create sheltered edges, shaded walkways and informal places to gather. Brick, timber screens and planted courtyards give the campus a tactile, climate-responsive character while keeping the scale approachable for children.

THE CAMPUS

Learning happens
between buildings.

The school is organised as a series of clusters around shared outdoor rooms. Instead of treating circulation as leftover space, the paths, steps and planted courtyards become active parts of the learning environment.

This creates a campus that can be read at a child's scale: short walks, visible destinations, shaded edges and places to pause. The landscape is not decoration around the buildings; it is part of the school's daily life.

SITE PLAN

Clusters around
a shared ground.

The plan separates the school into distinct curved building clusters while keeping them connected through a continuous landscape. Courts, play fields and planted pockets sit between the built volumes, allowing different age groups and activities to coexist.

SITE / LANDSCAPE / MOVEMENT

INSIDE / OUT

The classroom
opens outward.

Learning spaces are visually connected to the courtyards through large openings, shaded thresholds and timber screens. The boundary between classroom and garden is deliberately soft, allowing daylight, air and everyday activity to become part of the learning experience.

The interior is kept warm and familiar through timber, natural textures and child-scaled furniture.
